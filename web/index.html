What the numbers mean

Burning activity is the number of grid cells (0.1 degree, about 11 km) times days on which a satellite detected at least one vegetation-fire hotspot. One unit is one grid cell burning on one day. It measures how widespread burning was, not how intense.

Harmonized means the sensor no longer matters. MODIS (1 km pixels, from 2000) and VIIRS (375 m pixels, from 2012) report very different numbers of hotspots for the same fires. Here every value is on the scale of the VIIRS S-NPP sensor: since 2012 the values come from VIIRS, and earlier years are converted from MODIS with factors measured over 2012 to 2021, when both flew. Tested on years held out from the fit, the converted MODIS record reproduces what VIIRS recorded within 1.5 percent (Canada) and 3.5 percent (Zambia) over the three test years together. Single years are less exact: up to about 8 percent off in Canada and 5 percent in Zambia, and a typical single month about 15 percent (Canada) and 5 percent (Zambia).

Normal is the median of the same calendar month over every complete year since 2001. A hotspot detection is a satellite pixel flagged as containing fire; it is not a count of fires. Months are in UTC. Pre-2012 values for small areas are less certain than for whole regions.
