## When work may start

The FAQ says: "Teams are not allowed to begin working on the challenges prior to the hackathon."

The Team Formation Guide is more specific: "participants should NOT begin working on their project until the hackathon begins on November 14, 2026 (9:00 a.m. local time) to ensure a fair and equitable process." The same guide encourages participants to "review each challenge statement and select one for your team project before the hackathon starts."

So reading the challenge, choosing it, registering and creating the team page are allowed before the start. Building the project is not. As of November 13 this repo holds only the problem statement, this file and the task board; it holds no solution code or data. The pages do not say whether a task plan counts as working on the project, and only the Local Lead can settle that. Do not start any research or build task before 9:00 a.m. Eastern on November 14.

**Local Lead ruling (email, November 13):** a rough, high-level plan with no code and no data is preparation and is allowed. Actual work on the solution (code, data, design, prototypes) starts at 9:00 a.m. Eastern on Saturday. The FIRMS data request counts as work and must be submitted at 9:00 a.m., not before. Setting up tools and refining the plan are suggested for the first hour while the archive delivery is pending. Judging is of the Project page on the Space Apps website plus the demo and materials linked there; no live presentation is required, and any optional Q&A will be announced on Discord.

The guide also says that "participants are not expected to submit a fully developed project at the end."

## Eligibility for Global Judging

A project that misses any of these is not judged globally.

- It responds to one official challenge. A team works on one challenge only, and a person is on one team only.
- Every member is registered for the 2026 event, is confirmed (not waitlisted) at the same Local Event or the Universal Event, and appears on the team's Members tab. Members cannot be added after submission closes.
- The team has at most six members. A Local Event team needs at least one, a Universal Event team at least two.
- Every required field on the Project page is complete.
- The demo is within its limit: seven slides or 30 seconds. "Projects that exceed these limits will not be eligible for Global Judging."
- The project page and all linked material are in English. A video may be spoken in another language if it has English subtitles.
- "Submit for Judging" is clicked before the deadline, with both checkboxes ticked:
  - the team has read the submission requirements and agrees to the Terms and Conditions and Privacy Policy;
  - the project is the team's original work, every external resource is listed in the References field, and no copyrighted material is used without permission.
- The team passes NASA's review for affiliation with China or a Chinese-owned entity, including universities. This is based on affiliation, not nationality.
- To be eligible for a Global Award, the project uses at least one NASA data source or resource. FIRMS satisfies this.

## Submission format

The submission is the team's Project page on spaceappschallenge.org. Judges review this page, and most of their evaluation rests on it rather than on the demo.

| Field | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Project Name | The name of the project |
| High-Level Project Summary | What was developed, how it addresses the challenge, why it matters |
| Link to Project Demo | A public link to a slide deck of at most seven slides (title and intro slides count) or a video of at most 30 seconds |
| Link to Final Project | A public link to the project: the deployed app or the code repository |
| Detailed Project Description | What it does, how it works, its benefits, what the team hopes to achieve, and the tools, languages and software used |
| Use of Super Intelligence (SI) | Whether AI tools were used and how. The 2026 pages call AI "Super Intelligence". Judges do not see this answer |
| NASA Data | The specific NASA data and resources used |
| Space Agency Partner Data and Other References | Every data source, resource and tool used, including code, text and images, even if open source |

Rules that apply to the links and files:

- Every link must open without a password, permission or registration. "If judges can't access your project, it can't be evaluated or judged." Test each link in a private browser window.
- The page accepts images only (JPG, PNG or GIF, up to 10 MB). Videos and documents must be hosted elsewhere and linked.
- For a newly created website, the guide recommends requesting a [FortiGuard Web Filter Classification](https://www.fortiguard.com/faq/wfratingsubmit) so judges' networks do not block it. This is optional.
- A slide deck must be in English and must show the team name, the names of the team members and the challenge selected.
- No image, video, audio or likeness of anyone under 18 may appear in the demo or the project.

## Licensing, sources and AI use

- **Our own work:** original content must be freely available without restriction or under an OSI-approved open source licence. With no licence stated, NASA distributes it under Apache 2.0.
- **Third-party materials:** anything not from NASA or a space agency partner must be freely and publicly available or OSI-licensed. Its licence must be identified in the documentation submitted with the project.
- **Citation:** every source used must be cited, even if open source. NASA can remove nominations and awards from teams that do not.
- **AI tools:** permitted. The guide says they should accelerate the work and should not be used to come up with the idea for solving the challenge. Where AI was used must be marked:
  - images and videos carry a visible watermark;
  - audio, code and data are acknowledged in descriptive text and metadata.
- **NASA branding:** AI-generated content may not use, contain or modify NASA logos, flags or mission identifiers.
- **Not allowed in a submission:** promotion of commercial products or services, personally identifiable information, proprietary information, or export-controlled information.

## Judging criteria

Each criterion is scored from 1 to 5 at every level of judging. No weighting is stated. The guide asks teams to give Presentation the same attention as the other four.

| Criterion | What a score of 5 requires | Tasks that deliver it |
|---|---|---|
| Impact | Addresses a significant problem and benefits a large audience | BURN-011, BURN-017, BURN-026 |
| Creativity | An original approach, or a meaningful improvement on existing ideas | BURN-006, BURN-007, BURN-012, BURN-014 |
| Validity | Scientifically sound, technically feasible, fully developed, usable | BURN-004, BURN-005, BURN-008, BURN-019, BURN-021, BURN-022 |
| Relevance | Directly addresses the challenge, with NASA data as a core component | BURN-001, BURN-002, BURN-016, BURN-018, and the coverage check in [`tasks/README.md`](../tasks/README.md) |
| Presentation | The challenge, the solution and its significance are clear to someone with no context. Scored on the demo and the full project | BURN-026, BURN-028, BURN-021 |
